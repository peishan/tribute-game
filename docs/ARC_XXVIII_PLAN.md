## Sera's parents (the author's answer, recorded)
Checked against the built chapters: in the disappearance arc (239-262) Sera's mother is mentioned once (ch240: "I'd like to see my mother and speak to the court") and never shown; her father is never mentioned there; the only other mentions are ch401's letter from home (signed "Mother", "Father says the new tea... "; little cousins) and an unnamed bearded Altan official in ch263 ("the Altan sovereign: unnamed, not built"). **The author's answer: her parents retired to a wandering life, so they are nowhere to be found; they may appear in future chapters after the epilogue, likely a next-generation arc.**
- **Consequence for XXVIII:** they are absent from Rheon's coronation and wedding by design. Nothing needs to be shown; a line saying they cannot be reached, or have sent word from the road, is enough.
- **Sera's first marriage (the author's clarification, matches the built chapters):** Sera married **Roc** as a political marriage (ch66-68: engaged, then wed before the court; "a union of duty, power and alliance"); it was **never consummated**; when Roc went into exile (ch73) **King Chadstone granted her a divorce decree**; the chapters show her holding "the Divorce Scroll" when she asks to join the party at ch87. So the earlier reading that "Roc" was a slip for Hugh was wrong, and the roadmap's Sera and Hugh arc is the story of a divorced Altan princess (never a wife in practice) and a king.
- **Timing, flagged but not decided:** the answer says her parents retired to a wandering life after her marriage to Roc. The built chapters show them still in the picture later: ch240 Sera says "I'd like to see my mother and speak to the court", and ch401's letter from "Mother" (and "Father says...") describes a lively palace and a Spring Festival. The reconciliations that keep everything canon: (a) they retired *after* ch401, in the time since, leaving the realm to a council with Rheon as Crown Prince, so Rheon's ascension in XXVIII completes the handover; or (b) the retirement was announced earlier but only settled after the letter. Option (a) is the simplest and is the working assumption; nothing in the game contradicts it. The unnamed bearded official of ch263 stays unnamed.
- **Theme for the Altan reform (XXVIII):** Rheon's reform of the chastity-based and caste-bound marriage rules also protects Sera, a twice-complicated case (a divorced princess, never truly married) who may one day marry the King of Tribute; the Altan court's attitude to her marriage history is a natural thing for the reform to answer, alongside Mirelle's.
- Ch263's unnamed bearded official and ch401's letter are not contradicted: the sovereign is simply still unseen after 263, and the 401 letter predates the retirement.
- Rheon is already king in the roadmap's phrasing ("Rheon ascends the throne during the active campaign"); if the parents retire at his ascension, XXVIII can show the handover as a quiet scene (or as narration) with no on-page parents.
