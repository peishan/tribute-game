## Sera's parents (the author's answer, recorded)
Checked against the built chapters: in the disappearance arc (239-262) Sera's mother is mentioned once (ch240: "I'd like to see my mother and speak to the court") and never shown; her father is never mentioned there; the only other mention is ch237's letter from home (signed "Mother", "Father says the new tea..."; little cousins; ch401 only has Sera writing back) and an unnamed bearded Altan official in ch263 ("the Altan sovereign: unnamed, not built"). **The author's answer: her parents retired to a wandering life, so they are nowhere to be found; they may appear in future chapters after the epilogue, likely a next-generation arc.**
- **Consequence for XXVIII:** they are absent from Rheon's coronation and wedding by design. Nothing needs to be shown; a line saying they cannot be reached, or have sent word from the road, is enough.
- **Sera's first marriage (the author's clarification, matches the built chapters):** Sera married **Roc** as a political marriage (ch66-68: engaged, then wed before the court; "a union of duty, power and alliance"); it was **never consummated**; when Roc went into exile (ch73) **King Chadstone granted her a divorce decree**; the chapters show her holding "the Divorce Scroll" when she asks to join the party at ch87. So the earlier reading that "Roc" was a slip for Hugh was wrong, and the roadmap's Sera and Hugh arc is the story of a divorced Altan princess (never a wife in practice) and a king.
- **Timing (settled by the author, replacing the earlier working assumption):** the parents' retirement need not wait until after ch401. **Correction: ch401 (Letters from Home) shows no letter from Sera's parents.** It shows only "Sera writes a long letter back to the Altan Kingdom" (she is safe, learning, misses home, has grown), which works whether the letter goes to the family, the court or the parents on the road. The letter from "Mother" (with "Father says the new tea..." and little cousins) is **ch237, A Letter from Altan**, which I had mislabelled. The timeline is now: Sera's parents are around when her marriage to Roc is arranged (ch66-68); once Rheon is securely Crown Prince and Sera's marriage appears settled they gradually withdraw into a disguised wandering life; after the divorce and her joining the party (ch87) Sera knows nothing of it; at ch237-240 she writes and receives letters through the household and expects to see her mother; at the palace (ch241-243) nobody of the old generation receives her, because the royal **uncle and aunt, the ministers and the council** keep the court (the unnamed bearded official of ch263 can be read as the uncle acting for the throne: unnamed, as built). XXVIII then completes the transition when Rheon formally ascends.
- **Ch237's letter, reconciled (flagged, an option, not decided):** it is signed "Mother" and describes the palace and the Spring Festival. Easiest readings that keep it canon: it was written in the Queen Mother's name by the household, or composed before she left and sent on by the court, or forwarded to Sera from the road by arrangement. The parents may also have asked the court not to tell Sera, so she could keep her own path. Nothing on the built pages says otherwise.
- **Payoff:** an epilogue or next-generation appearance in which the parents have been travelling as commoners while their children rebuilt Altan.
- **Theme for the Altan reform (XXVIII):** Rheon's reform of the chastity-based and caste-bound marriage rules also protects Sera, a twice-complicated case (a divorced princess, never truly married) who may one day marry the King of Tribute; the Altan court's attitude to her marriage history is a natural thing for the reform to answer, alongside Mirelle's.
- Ch263's unnamed bearded official is not contradicted (he can be the acting royal uncle) and ch401 needs no change.
- Rheon is already king in the roadmap's phrasing ("Rheon ascends the throne during the active campaign"); if the parents retire at his ascension, XXVIII can show the handover as a quiet scene (or as narration) with no on-page parents.
