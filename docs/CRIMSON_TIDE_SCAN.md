# Crimson Tide scan: what can carry over to Tribute

Source: peishan/daybreak-crimson-tide (read only; nothing copied). Scan 1: Bonds / Relationships.
Files read: scripts/arc9-and-systems.js (bond tracks), scripts/arc16-and-bonding.js (disagreement and repair), scripts/hang-out-*.js (activities and Shared Moments), scripts/settlement-memories.js, scripts/character-bios.js.

## How Crimson Tide does bonds
- A bond is a TRACK, not a per-character number: san_joel (a pair), san_crew (a headcount: any 3+ crew fielded), san_trio (three named members), and one per companion (Aisyah, Mez, Eliz, Senedra, Joy). Each track has its own ladder of 5 named tiers (e.g. "Strangers Still" -> "Getting to Know Them" -> "Trusted Hands" -> "Found Family" -> "This Is Home") and thresholds 0/50/150/300/600.
- One action per bond per day, 15 points each. Progress always accrues; the BONUS is a synergy that only applies while the bonded members are actually in the fielded party. Bonuses are small (2-8%) and flow through one aggregator (getReputationBonus) so no new combat hooks are needed. Individual companion tracks carry NO combat bonus on purpose: story-driven, not stat grinding.
- Hang Out: a menu of flavoured activities (Coffee, Walk the Harbour, Night Market, Stay In...) that all feed the same daily cap and the same points, plus a Shared Moments log (a memory collection, not a second score) that records which activities actually happened.
- Disagreement and Repair (San and Joel): a state machine Connected -> Tension -> Understanding -> Repaired. Disagreement NEVER subtracts points: bonding measures how well people understand each other, not how often they agree. The player picks a response (push, listen, walk away), can ask each crew member for their perspective, builds "understanding points", then takes repair steps; a repaired disagreement leaves a permanent "Learned" flag that later dialogue can check. Perspectives per character are stored as data so extending to other pairs is data entry.
- Settlement Memories: story events permanently add small descriptive details to the home port, each tied to real game state, revealed once, and logged in a Chronicle.
- Character bios: image sheets unlocked when ALL depicted characters have joined (generic, not hard-coded arc numbers).

## Tribute today (for comparison)
Companion bonds: bond points with Jade only, 6 levels, bond skills in BONDTREE, daily "spend time" actions in some places. New Bonds tab: allies and family with standing 0-200, six tiers, four gestures a day, favours that are global (fares, gold/XP/renown), no negative moves except CH_REL.

## Cautions seen in their code
- Bonuses are deliberately small and mostly conditional. Our new ally favours stack globally; keep them small and consider making some synergy-conditional.
- They hit bugs from wrapping window functions across files (a render wrapper that never ran). We use plain functions in one module, so we avoid it, but any extension should extend data tables, not wrap.

## Scoping rules for future scans (author)
- Crimson Tide is a Tradewinds-style trading and sailing game; Tribute is a fantasy JRPG. Carry over only what suits a story-driven party RPG; skip trade, fleet, ship, harbour-economy, route and market systems unless they can be reframed (e.g. rumour market -> Sally's rumours).
- Crimson Tide's birthdays, real-calendar events and festivals are based on real people, real dates and real countries. Do NOT bring them to Tribute: Tribute is a fantasy land, and its names (people, places, kingdoms) are invented. Dropped from the candidate list: character-birthdays, real-calendar-events, festival-drops, anything keyed to the real calendar or real-world places. If Tribute ever gets seasonal or festival content it must be in-world and invented, never tied to real dates or countries.
- Still-open candidates (reframed for a fantasy RPG): chronicle (an in-world journal), settlement/home memories, port standing -> standing per region/town, rumour market -> Sally's rumour network, quest tracker (we have one), bestiary, achievements, crafting, raid mode (we have Guardian Raid), crew conversations and hobbies (idle banter and hang outs).

## Scan 3: loot tables and chapter level gates (built)
What Crimson Tide actually does: (a) chapter gates are a data field on a chapter, {type:'level', value, label}, checked in a single objective-state function, and used on only ONE chapter (Arc II ch14, level 50, "Reach level 50 to reflect on how far the crew has come"); vessels and traders use minLevel the same way. (b) Loot is boss-name themes: keywords in a boss's name pick an icon and an item base (Sea Beast Scale, Spectral Essence, Pirate Trophy...), and each theme icon maps to a trinket with a small bonus (+8% damage, -12% damage taken, +20 max MP).
Brought to Tribute (loot.js, gates.js):
- Chapter level gates: milestone chapters stay locked until the party's AVERAGE level (real members only; passive companions and guests not counted) reaches chapter x 0.6 (before ch50) or x 0.65. Gated: battle chapters, arc and hunt openers (43, 87, 104, 122, 147, 150, 153, 157) and every tenth chapter from 60 (26 chapters now). The locked chapter shows its title with "Average party level N needed (now M)" in the Journal and in place of the Next chapter button; completed chapters never lock. Story-only play stays ahead of the gates until about ch130, then falls 2-5 levels short, which side content (investigations, hunts, Skirmish) covers. Tuning: GATE.early / GATE.late / GATE_OPENERS in gates.js.
- Area loot: every won hunt or ambush (not story battles, not bosses) also rolls the loot table of the place where it was fought (AREA_LOOT, 13 places so far), with chapter-gated rows. 12 new materials, 10 new pieces of gear (accessories, cloaks, a boss-drop veil and blade) and 8 new recipes for Jenika. Bosses keep their LOOT tables and also roll a name-themed item (LOOT_THEMES), and the last two bosses that had no table (guardian spirit, spirit core) now have one: every boss has a loot table. Bestiary tab now opens with "Where to find things" for places you have visited. Names, chances and bonuses are first-pass and invented.

## Scan 4: achievements, crew conversations, hobbies, tide stories, mood, time of day, crafting, bestiary, raid mode
Files read: achievements.js (656 lines, 243 entries), fair-tide-crew-conversations.js, fair-tide-hobbies.js, tide-stories.js, fair-tide-mood.js, fair-tide-time-of-day.js, crafting.js, bestiary.js, raid-mode.js.

How Crimson Tide does them
- Achievements: pure recognition, no mechanical reward. Each is {id, name, icon, desc, check()} and check() only reads state that already exists (a chapter flag, a bond tier, a recruit, a discovery flag). One loop checks them all on every main-goal render; an earned one toasts, logs and opens a small modal; the screen shows earned/total, with unearned ones hidden as "???". 243 entries, mostly one per story chapter beat plus recruits, discoveries, bond ladders and capstones.
- Crew conversations ("Overheard"): tiny scripted exchanges (3-4 lines) between named characters, each eligible only when every speaker has joined; a 35% chance per new day, at most one, no reward.
- Hobbies: one-line comic moments per character ("Renn was reported missing. He was in the Archive."), same eligibility and chance, no reward.
- Tide Stories: a thin composition layer: a plain array of sources {id, slot (morning/afternoon/evening), eligible(), generate()}; any system registers its own source, and each day it rolls one story per slot and logs it. That is the extension point that lets hobbies, conversations, weather and memories surface in one place.
- Mood / weather / time of day: all keyed to the REAL clock and calendar (a 0-4% XP flavour meter fed by festivals, weather and flavour events). Not applicable to Tribute (real dates are out of scope).
- Crafting: found that most trophies were clutter, so every trophy type now spends into real gear through the existing gear pipeline; the entry-level recipes eat the generic trophy. Lesson: every drop needs a use.
- Bestiary: completion count visible, undiscovered entries as "???" but still counted.
- Raid Mode: a generic array of tiered raids (sequential stages, partial recovery between them, first-clear vs reduced repeat rewards); Tribute already has the Guardian Raid, which could take this shape.

What fits Tribute (ranked)
1. Deeds (achievements): HIGH. Data-driven recognition with check() over state we already keep: every chapter milestone (155 chapters, we can generate most from CHAPTER_DESIGN), each Evil resolved, bond/track tiers, regard tiers, homes, bosses beaten, gear crafted, guests met, gates cleared. No rewards (matches genre and keeps balance clean). Hidden until earned.
2. Overheard (crew conversations) and Moments (hobbies) as the party's quiet life: HIGH, and they extend our idle banter: short scripted exchanges and one-line comic moments per character, eligible only when everyone in them has joined, shown in Listen to the party / at homes / at camps, logged to the Chronicle. Needs writing in your voice; I would draft a first set from what the comics already established.
3. A Story Sources layer: MEDIUM. Our Chronicle already collects events; add a plain registry so overheard lines, whispers, home memories and regard changes can all register one "today" line (a recap strip on the Journal) instead of separate screens.
4. Raid ladder: MEDIUM. Turn the Guardian Raid into a data array of tiers with partial recovery between stages; later raids can be tied to the sealed regions (post-Arc V).
5. Bestiary completion count and "every drop has a use": LOW effort, mostly done (loot pass); add the discovered/total line and a trophy-use check.
Skipped (real-world dependent or trade): mood, weather, time-of-day, festivals, birthdays; port visitors and ship personality (trade/ship systems).

## Scan 5: the Fair Tide settlement (buildings, residents, rival disposition) and the renown ladder
Files read: fairtide-buildings-and-arc6.js (2,220 lines), fairtide-systems.js, rival-disposition.js (headers and data).
How Crimson Tide does it
- Fair Tide is a home settlement the player rebuilds: a Port HQ plus six buildings (warehouse, trading post, galley, workshop, watchtower, archive). Each has its own level, a cost that scales with the next level (base x level), a cap tied to the HQ's level, and named tiers ("A Cookfire" -> "The Galley Everyone Remembers"). Each building is staffed by a named companion who joins the roster when it is built.
- A four-resource economy (timber, stone, food, trade goods) earned by "gathering party" dispatches (timed runs). Buildings turn those into effects on systems that already exist, rather than new mechanics: cheaper upgrades, better prices, faster research, a story XP bonus at the Archive's tiers.
- A resident roster: named residents and generic freed workers each add a small gold or XP bonus through one aggregator (1-2% each), not a new combat slot.
- Rival disposition: once a captured rival's service ends, a one-time choice: fine and release them (+200 gold), or take them in as a resident (a small bonus). Hands off anyone whose fate belongs to a planned story beat.
- A reputation rank ladder (renown ranks) with small XP/gold/crit bonuses at each rank.
What fits Tribute (ranked)
1. The Estates: Gold Manor and Devon's Palace as upgradeable homes (we already have free rest, meals, stash, memories). Rooms or wings with levels, paid for with the materials the new area loot finally gives a use (frontier hide, moonroot, spirit thread, seal dust, thorn resin) plus gold; effects on systems we already have: an Apothecary (free tonics from herbs), a Workshop (cheaper crafting), a Study or Archive (investigation XP and a Chronicle recap), Stables (cheaper travel), a Training Yard (small daily XP). Each wing named for the person who tends it (Jenika's pavilion exists; Haren; Liora's room). Needs your names and tiers.
2. Renown ranks (Phoenix Guard ranks): Tribute already tracks renown. A ladder of ranks (named for the guard, not real ranks) with small XP/gold bonuses, shown on the Missions panel and a Deed per rank.
3. Residents and aftermath: when places are saved or Evils are resolved (the Hart's valley, the frontier villages), a few named residents can come to live at the estates and add small bonuses or flavour. Reframes rival disposition: no prisoners, instead what becomes of the people and places you helped.
4. Errand runs (timed gathering): CT dispatches parties on real-time timers. Tribute has the Rewards tab's AFK income; a game-day errand system for the household staff is possible later but is the least needed.
Skipped: trade goods, ships, shipyard upgrades, community service for captured pirates.
