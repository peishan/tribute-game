The emphasis is saving Jenika, not a romantic reward (handled tastefully, off-page). Jenika survives; later learns she is pregnant, so the child is genuinely older by the final Evil. Aster privately releases Elise from the political marriage (the frontier is stable); Elise chooses Tarin; the arc covers her departure with a **faked death from illness (45 days of mourning) while Fen, in a wig and the same height, poses as Elise, and an unannounced Aster/Fen wedding, announced 90 to 100 days later** (full plan: docs/ARC_XXIV_PLAN.md); if pregnancy is used it speeds her departure but does not cause the choice; they leave for the secluded life. ## Late-game political reforms (planned; subject to change like the rest)
Two clean reforms, distinct in tone, giving both men a legacy beyond who they marry.
- **Aster: the House Xuanji / Dragonvale reform.** After he marries Fen, he abolishes the royal polygamous marriage structure. Aim: end the household system that encouraged competing consort factions, succession rivalry and instability inside the royal family. Top down: he changes the structure that shaped his own family. (Fits the foster-rearing and separate-households history of ch376-384.)
- **Rheon: the Altan reform.** He challenges the region's chastity-based social rules and caste-bound marriage restrictions: no one's worth or marriage prospects should be set by sexual history, "purity" or rigid class and caste lines. Broader socially: it reaches ordinary people as well as nobles. It is also the structure that would otherwise have made his relationship with Mirelle, and many others, socially difficult or impossible (XXVIII).
Placement: Rheon's belongs with XXVIII (coronation and marriage). Aster's follows his marriage to Fen (after XXIV, when Fen becomes his recognised partner), likely announced in a later arc or shown as aftermath; to be set when the chapters are drawn. Keep politics light, per the author's direction that the focus is the remaining Evils and true love for everyone.
